# SPX volatility surface: SVI, SSVI, eSSVI, arbitrage-free SVI and how much arbitrage is real

and are regenerated by `make all` (or `python -m volsurf run data/raw/20261017T212629Z`).

**Delay.** Yahoo option quotes run about 15 minutes behind during market hours. This
snapshot was taken on a weekend, so it holds the last quotes of Friday's session. The
quote time is an estimate: Yahoo publishes no quote timestamps, only last-*trade*
timestamps, and many strikes last traded days or weeks ago. Exchange holidays are not
modelled when choosing the quote time.

**Settlement.** On monthly dates Yahoo lists both the AM-settled SPX series and the
PM-settled SPXW series. For each expiry the pipeline keeps the series with more quoted
strikes (SPX on every monthly date here) and uses its settlement time: 09:30 ET for
SPX, 16:00 ET for SPXW. T is measured in calendar time (ACT/365) from the quote time
to settlement.

A second, earlier snapshot (`20261017T212354Z`) used the 2026-11-19 Thursday SPXW
weekly instead of the 2026-11-20 monthly, because of a bug in expiry selection that
has since been fixed. It is kept because raw data is never deleted, and its results
agree closely with this one.

### Forwards and discount factors

Each expiry gets one weighted regression of C − P on K over strikes near the money,
with weights 1 / (call spread + put spread) and a second pass that drops residuals
beyond 3 MADs. The implied rates come out at 4.5–5.2% for every SPX expiry.

Expiries under 0.08 years and SPXW slices are the exception. Their discount factor is
taken from the SPX parity curve, log-linear in T and flat beyond the ends, and only F is
fitted, with the slope fixed at −D. Here that is the 2-week SPXW expiry, which gets 4.48%,
extrapolated flat from 2026-11-20. Its own parity slope is not usable on this feed. It
gave 3.8%, and depending on the strike band it ranges from −7% to 3.8%, by many standard
errors, so C − P has small systematic curvature in K at two weeks. SPXW series also give
lower rates than SPX on every shared date (2.7% vs 4.5% at 2026-11-20), and on the live
snapshots the 2-week parity rate swung from 5.8% to 1.7% within a day. Borrowing D moves
the 2-week forward by 0.002 index points and its vols by under 0.01 vol points, so the
surface does not notice. `forwards.csv` records each expiry's `D_source`, and `volsurf
run --parity-d` restores the parity-only fit. Details are in
[docs/robustness.md](docs/robustness.md), section 4. Implied carry (r − q) is 3.3–4.2%.
The residual standard deviation is 0.07–0.24 index points; the 2028 expiry is the
noisiest at 0.65.

## Raw quote arbitrage

Model-free checks on the OTM quote set, each run twice: on mid prices, and on
executable prices (buy at the ask, sell at the bid). For strike checks, OTM puts are
expressed as calls through put-call parity. Parity adds the same linear function of K
to bid and ask, so butterflies are unaffected, and call checks on converted puts are
exactly the corresponding put checks. Only the pair or triple that straddles F
depends on the estimated forward. Butterflies use strike-gap weights, because
spacing is uneven (5, 10, 25 and 50 point grids all appear).

These are far-dated, rarely traded strikes. A quote there is most likely stale or

would call tradable** without a live, firm quote on the zero-open-interest strike.

The same six survivors appear under every cleaning threshold tested (spread filter
0.3–0.8 of mid, ITM cut 5–20%), with the same profits. Their spreads are under 30% of
mid, so no spread filter removes them; only an open-interest filter would.

**Live check.** That "all zero open interest" pattern is specific to this snapshot.
Three live intraday snapshots on Tuesday 2026-11-03 give 27–34 survivors each, and
21–23 of them have a zero-open-interest leg. Those carry the large "profits" (up to
$9,355) on mis-marked 2028-06-16 strikes. The rest are $5–$78 two-week SPXW
butterflies that move from strike to strike between runs, which is what
unsynchronised, 15-minute-delayed quotes look like, plus three structures on one
inverted OI-18 put. See `docs/robustness.md`, section 1.

**The ITM diagnostic.** Running the same strike checks on every clean quote, including
in-the-money strikes up to 10% ITM, gives 908 mid violations and 126 survivors, with
"profits" up to $22,700 per contract. The survivor count depends on how deep into the
money you go: 18 at a 5% ITM cut, 126 at 10%, 290 at 20%. These are plainly bad data. For example, the
Feb-2027 8350 put is quoted 530.4/532.8 while the 8325 put is 601.1/606.9, and the
8350 has zero open interest. ITM quotes in the Yahoo feed are unreliable, which is a
second reason (besides liquidity) to build the surface from OTM options only.

## Fits

All fits minimise weighted squared error in total variance. Weights are the inverse
of the bid-ask spread in total variance. For SSVI and eSSVI each slice's weights are normalised
to mean 1 and its residuals are scaled by that slice's ATM total variance, so every
expiry counts about equally in vol terms. That scaling doesn't change the optimum of
a single SVI slice, so both models minimise the same objective.

* **SVI**: raw parameterisation, quasi-explicit calibration (Zeliade 2009). For fixed
  (m, σ) the slice is linear in (a, bσ, ρbσ), solved by constrained weighted least
  squares. Only (m, σ) are searched, with Nelder–Mead from 12 starts. Constraints:
  b ≥ 0, |ρ| < 1, σ > 0, a + bσ√(1−ρ²) ≥ 0, and b(1+|ρ|) ≤ 1.999. The last one keeps
  both asymptotic wing slopes, b(1±ρ), strictly below Lee's bound of 2.
* **SSVI**: power-law φ(θ) = η / (θ^γ (1+θ)^(1−γ)). One global (ρ, η, γ) plus one θ
  per expiry, fitted jointly with bounded least squares. No-arbitrage holds by
  construction: η = 2s/(1+|ρ|) with s ∈ (0, 1], γ ∈ (0, ½], and θ is a cumulative sum
  of non-negative increments.
* **SSVI (unconstrained)**: same, with γ ∈ (0, 1) and no η bound. This run is only a
  diagnostic, to measure what the constraints cost.
* **eSSVI**: the SSVI slice with its own (θ, ρ, ψ = θφ) per expiry, 30 parameters in
  all, fitted jointly. It imposes the eSSVI calendar conditions between adjacent slices
  (θ and ψ non-decreasing, ψ₂ ≥ ψ₁·max((1+ρ₁)/(1+ρ₂), (1−ρ₁)/(1−ρ₂)), and φ
  non-increasing) and GJ14's butterfly bounds on each slice. A reparametrisation maps a
  unit box onto exactly that set, so bounded least squares enforces it. Sources, a
  counterexample showing that the weaker calendar set alone is not sufficient, and the
  tests are in [docs/essvi.md](docs/essvi.md).
* **SVI (arb-free)**: Gatheral and Jacquier's §5.2 recipe. A square-root SSVI fit
  (φ = η/√θ) supplies the starting guess. Raw SVI is then refitted one slice at a time
  from the shortest expiry, 50 parameters in all. Each slice's objective adds two heavy
  penalties: one on any shortfall below the slice refitted just before it
  (crossedness) and one on g(k) < 0. Both are evaluated on a k grid that contains the
  report grid and extends coarsely to ±15. The wing slopes b(1 ± ρ) may not fall from one
  expiry to the next and stay at or below 1.999. Both are imposed exactly, as box
  bounds. The butterfly penalty is not part of GJ14 §5.2, and the no-arbitrage result is
  a grid check rather than a property of the parameterisation. Details are in
  [docs/svi_arbfree.md](docs/svi_arbfree.md).

### Fitted arbitrage

A slice is free of butterfly arbitrage if and only if g(k) ≥ 0 for every k *and*
d₊(k) → −∞ as k → +∞ (GJ14 Lemma 2.2). The second condition holds exactly when the
right wing's asymptotic total-variance slope is below Lee's bound of 2. Both are
checked: g(k) on a grid k ∈ [−2.5, 1.0], and the wing slope analytically. Calendar
arbitrage is checked by whether adjacent fitted slices cross (w at T₂ below w at T₁)
on the same grid. Adjacent pairs are enough, because non-crossing is transitive.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SVI | 50 | 0.16 | 5 of 10 | 0 | 0 | 6 of 9 (6–7 across thresholds) | 0 (weekend snapshot) |
| SSVI | 13 | 3.00 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| SSVI unconstrained | 13 | 1.93 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| eSSVI | 30 | 2.20 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| SVI arb-free | 50 | 0.24 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

**A correction.** The first version of this pipeline bounded SVI wings at
b(1+|ρ|) ≤ 4. That was wrong: in total variance, Lee's moment formula caps the
asymptotic slope at 2 (GJ14 Remark 4.3), and Lemma 2.2 needs the right slope strictly
below 2. Under the old bound, 7 of the 10 SVI slices had right-wing slopes near 4.
By the full Lemma 2.2 test, 9 of 10 slices then had butterfly arbitrage, not the 5
originally reported. With the corrected bound, the same 7 slices sit on it (slope
1.999), and RMSE moves from 0.163 to 0.164 vol points. The data barely constrain the
far call wing, so tightening it costs almost nothing in fit.

On this snapshot, every SVI arbitrage sits on the **call wing, beyond the last quoted strike**. g turns
negative from k = 0.31 to 0.96 onward, depending on the slice (min g = −0.92 on 2027-01-15).
Slices cross from k = 0.16 to 0.51 onward. The cause is visible in the parameters:
for nine of ten expiries SVI puts its vertex to the right of the data (m = 0.19–1.01,
against a last quoted k of 0.07–0.41) with ρ > 0. On this snapshot the quoted smile is
still falling at the last liquid call, so the data never pins the right wing. SVI is
free to bend it however it likes. Seven slices push the wing all the way to the
Lee bound, and it bends hard. Inside the quoted range the SVI surface is clean, but as soon as you
extrapolate, for pricing or for a density, it is not. On two of the three live
snapshots one pair (2027-02-19 → 2027-03-19) crosses from k ≈ 0.21, about 0.01 inside
the last quoted call, so the clean-inside-the-data result holds only approximately.

## The trade-off

**SVI fits each smile 16–19 times more tightly than SSVI (0.16 vs 3.00 vol points at
the default cleaning; SSVI ranges from 2.6 to 3.3 as the spread filter goes from 0.3 to
0.8, while SVI stays at 0.16–0.18; on the live snapshots it is 0.25–0.26 vs 3.0–3.2,
or 12–13×)
and keeps 80% of fitted vols inside the bid-ask spread, against 9% for SSVI (28–31%
against 2–3% on the tighter live intraday spreads). But half
of the SVI slices imply a negative density, and two thirds of adjacent expiry pairs
cross, all in the extrapolated call wing on this snapshot. SSVI uses 13 parameters instead of 50 and is
arbitrage-free everywhere by construction, at the cost of missing the short-dated
put wing by several vol points. eSSVI, with 30 parameters, keeps the guarantee and
closes about a quarter of the gap, to 2.20 (2.38–2.57 live). Arbitrage-free SVI
(GJ14 §5.2), with 50 parameters, removes every fitted arbitrage SVI has and lands at
0.24 (0.33–0.36 live), with 62% of fitted vols inside the spread (17–20% live). That
recovers 96% of the eSSVI–SVI gap on every snapshot, but its no-arbitrage is checked
on a grid, not guaranteed by construction.**

Where SSVI's 2.8-point gap to SVI comes from. Fitting the same SSVI slice to each expiry
on its own separates the pieces:

| Fit | RMSE (vol pts) | What it adds |
|---|---:|---|
| SVI, per slice | 0.16 | five parameters per slice, no calendar conditions |
| SVI, per slice, g ≥ 0 | 0.24 | GJ14 §5.2 refit with the butterfly penalty only |
| SVI arb-free | 0.24 | plus the crossedness penalty and wing-slope order between slices |
| SSVI slice, per expiry, no constraints | 1.91 | three parameters per slice, no conditions at all |
| SSVI slice, per expiry, GJ butterfly bounds | 2.20 | the sufficient butterfly bounds |
| eSSVI | 2.20 | plus the eSSVI calendar conditions between slices |
| SSVI, constrained | 3.00 | plus one ρ and one φ(θ) shared by every expiry |

* About **1.75 points** is the SSVI slice shape itself. With three parameters, its
  minimum, curvature and wings are tied together through (θ, ρ, ψ), and it cannot match
  the short-dated SPX smiles that raw SVI fits with five. No choice of conditions
  removes this part.
* About **0.3 points** comes from GJ14's butterfly bounds, which are sufficient but not
  necessary.
* The eSSVI calendar conditions **cost nothing measurable**: eSSVI reaches the
  per-slice butterfly-only optimum to within 0.01 vol points.
* About **0.8 points** comes from sharing one ρ and one φ(θ) across maturities, together
  with the power-law constraints. That is the part eSSVI removes.
* Keeping the five-parameter SVI slice and imposing no-arbitrage directly costs
  **0.08 points** (0.08–0.10 live), and all of it is the butterfly penalty. Adding the
  calendar penalty and the wing-slope order changes the RMSE by under 0.002 on every
  snapshot, and in fact lowers it slightly, which is a difference of local optima.
  SVI's crossings all sit beyond the quotes, and once each call wing is made
  butterfly-free they are gone. The cost lands where the arbitrage was. g(k) ≥ 0 binds just beyond the last
  quoted call on most slices, where the fitted density is pushed to about zero, and
  the fit gives up about 0.1 point inside the quotes to get there. (Dropping the
  butterfly penalty and keeping only the calendar one gives 0.73, or 0.49–0.56 on the
  other snapshots, worse than both. Without g ≥ 0, early slices push their call wings to Lee's bound, and the wing-slope
  order then locks every later slice to it. This is an artefact of the greedy
  slice-by-slice order, not a cost of calendar arbitrage.)

An earlier version of this report put about 1.9 points down to the single ρ and φ, on
the grounds that relaxing SSVI's constraints left 1.93. That was the wrong reading. The
unconstrained SSVI fit, which still shares ρ and φ, lands within 0.02 points of the
per-slice SSVI fit with no constraints at all, so sharing the parameters costs little
once the constraints are off. The 1.9 points come from the slice family. The live
snapshots show the same split; see [docs/essvi.md](docs/essvi.md).

The unconstrained fit lands at γ = 0.63, η(1+|ρ|) = 1.41. That breaks the sufficient
butterfly condition θφ²(1+|ρ|) ≤ 4 for θ < 0.015, which covers the seven shortest
expiries; the worst value at a fitted θ is 10.5. Even so, a direct check finds g ≥ 0
on k ∈ [−15, 15] for every fitted slice. On this snapshot the unconstrained surface
happens to be arbitrage-free. But that is something you learn by checking after the
fit, not something the parameterisation guarantees. The constrained fit is
guaranteed.

If you need a smile that matches quotes for marking or for spotting quote-level
relative value, SVI per slice is still the tightest fit, but its wings must be fixed or
checked before any extrapolation. Arbitrage-free SVI is that fix. GJ14 §5.2 with a
butterfly penalty costs 0.08 vol points against raw SVI, against about 2 for eSSVI and
2.8 for SSVI. That makes it the better choice for a surface to interpolate, take
densities from or feed to a local-vol model, with two caveats. First, its
no-arbitrage is checked on k ∈ [−15, 15] (spacing 0.001 on [−4, 2]) plus exact
wing-slope bounds. It holds on the grid but is not proved between grid points, whereas
SSVI and eSSVI hold by construction.
On this snapshot the slack is thin: the smallest g on the report grid is 1.4 × 10⁻⁶ and
the smallest gap between adjacent slices is 5 × 10⁻⁹. Second, the far call wing is
shaped by the g ≥ 0 bound, not by quotes. Where a guarantee by construction is worth
about 2 vol points of fit, eSSVI remains the safe choice. Not tried here: GJ14's price
objective in place of total variance, penalising against the next slice as well as the
previous one, and a necessary-and-sufficient butterfly condition for SSVI slices, which
might close part of eSSVI's 0.3 points. With 96% of the gap recovered, none of these
looks worth much.

## No-arbitrage conditions used for SSVI

From Gatheral and Jacquier (2014), *Arbitrage-free SVI volatility surfaces*,
Quantitative Finance 14(1). Checked against the SSRN version dated 17 March 2013
(ssrn.com/abstract=2033323):

1. **Calendar** (Thm 4.1, necessary and sufficient): θ_t is non-decreasing in t, and
   0 ≤ ∂_θ(θφ(θ)) ≤ (1/ρ²)(1 + √(1−ρ²)) φ(θ) for all θ > 0, with no upper bound when ρ = 0.
2. **Butterfly** (Thm 4.2, sufficient): θφ(θ)(1+|ρ|) < 4 and θφ(θ)²(1+|ρ|) ≤ 4 for all θ > 0.
   Lemma 4.2 shows the first condition is necessary and the second is tight.

For the power law φ(θ) = η θ^(−γ) (1+θ)^(γ−1):

* θφ = η (θ/(1+θ))^(1−γ), which is increasing in θ and below η. So ∂_θ(θφ) ≥ 0, and
  ∂_θ(θφ)/φ = (1−γ)/(1+θ) < 1 ≤ (1+√(1−ρ²))/ρ². Calendar therefore needs only
  monotone θ.
* θφ(1+|ρ|) < η(1+|ρ|) ≤ 2 < 4.
* θφ² = η² (θ/(1+θ))^(1−2γ) / (1+θ) ≤ η² when γ ≤ ½. So θφ²(1+|ρ|) ≤ η²(1+|ρ|) ≤
  4/(1+|ρ|) ≤ 4 when η(1+|ρ|) ≤ 2.

The project brief's conditions (γ ∈ (0, ½], η(1+|ρ|) ≤ 2, θ increasing) therefore
imply both theorems. This φ is the paper's equation (4.5) in Remark 4.4, which says it
gives a surface "completely free of static arbitrage provided that η(1+|ρ|) ≤ 2"; the
SSRN text states no restriction on γ at that point. The derivation above needs γ ≤ ½
for the second butterfly condition: for γ > ½, θφ² grows without bound as θ → 0. That
matches the paper's own analysis of the pure power law in the same remark, and it is
exactly what the unconstrained fit (γ = 0.63) runs into. `tests/test_ssvi.py` also confirms this numerically:

* for 20 random admissible parameter sets, g ≥ 0 and slices never cross;
* fits to data generated outside the admissible region always return admissible
  parameters;
* parameters outside the region do produce g < 0.

## Limitations

* **Snapshot dependence.** The headline numbers come from one weekend snapshot
  (Friday 2026-10-16 close). Three live snapshots on Tuesday 2026-11-03 (10:30,
  12:45 and 15:24 ET; quotes delayed about 15 minutes) give 661–729 mid violations,
  zero raw calendar violations, SVI RMSE 0.25–0.26, SSVI RMSE 3.0–3.2, eSSVI RMSE
  2.38–2.57 and arbitrage-free SVI RMSE 0.33–0.36, so the SVI/SSVI gap holds at 12–13× instead of 19×. The survivor count does not hold:
  27–34 violations survive bid/ask live, against 6 on the weekend. 21–23 of them
  have a zero-open-interest leg and carry the large "profits" (up to $9,355 per
  contract) on mis-marked 2028-06-16 strikes. The rest are $5–$78 two-week SPXW
  butterflies on 5- and 10-point strikes that change from run to run, consistent
  with unsynchronised delayed quotes, plus three structures on one inverted
  OI-18 put. The 2-week SPXW parity rate moved from 5.8% to 1.7% within the day,
  which is why short and SPXW discount factors are now borrowed from the SPX curve.
  Fitted SVI calendar crossings reached about 0.01 into the quoted call wing on
  one pair (2027-02-19 → 2027-03-19) in two of the three runs. See
  `docs/robustness.md`, section 1.
* **Quote quality.** Yahoo bid and ask carry no timestamps or sizes, so "survives bid
  and ask" means "survives the displayed quotes", not "was executable".
* **Narrow call wing.** After cleaning, OTM calls reach only k ≈ 0.05–0.41. That is
  exactly why SVI's right wing is unconstrained.
* **Calendar check interpolation.** Raw calendar checks interpolate the longer expiry's
  total variance linearly in k between neighbouring quotes no more than 0.05 apart. It
  found no violations, even on mid. An audit confirms that this zero is real. The check
  made 2,088 comparisons (98.6% of shorter-expiry quotes), the tightest one still had
  7.5% more total variance at the longer expiry, and an injected violation is detected.
  Zero is expected for expiries three weeks or more apart.
* **Fitted checks are grid-based.** k ∈ [−2.5, 1.0] with 3,501 points; the wider
  [−15, 15] check was run once for the SSVI fits. For arbitrage-free SVI the grid is
  also what the fit enforces, so its no-arbitrage rests on the grid alone.
* **Rates.** Discount factors come from parity, not from an external OIS curve. SPX
  expiries use their own parity regression (4.5–5.2%). Expiries under 0.08 years and
  SPXW slices borrow from that curve, because their own parity rate is not identified on
  this feed; see the forwards section.
