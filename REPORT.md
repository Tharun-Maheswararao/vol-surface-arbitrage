**Delay.** Yahoo option quotes run about 15 minutes behind during market hours. This
snapshot was taken on a weekend, so it holds the last quotes of Friday's session. The
quote time is an estimate: Yahoo publishes no quote timestamps, only last-*trade*
timestamps, and many strikes last traded days or weeks ago. Exchange holidays are not
modelled when choosing the quote time.

**Settlement.** On monthly dates Yahoo lists both the AM-settled SPX series and the
PM-settled SPXW series. For each expiry the pipeline keeps the series with more quoted
strikes (SPX on every monthly date here) and uses its settlement time: 09:30 ET for
SPX, 16:00 ET for SPXW. T is measured in calendar time (ACT/365) from the quote time
to settlement.

A second, earlier snapshot (`20261017T212354Z`) used the 2026-11-19 Thursday SPXW
weekly instead of the 2026-11-20 monthly, because of a bug in expiry selection that
has since been fixed. It is kept because raw data is never deleted, and its results

### Forwards and discount factors

Each expiry gets one weighted regression of C − P on K over strikes near the money,
with weights 1 / (call spread + put spread) and a second pass that drops residuals
beyond 3 MADs. The implied rates come out at 4.5–5.2% for every expiry past one
month. The 2-week expiry gives 3.8%, which is expected: at T = 0.04 a parity residual
of a few cents moves the implied rate a lot. Implied carry (r − q) is 3.3–4.2%. The residual
standard deviation is 0.07–0.24 index points; the 2028 expiry is the noisiest at
0.65.

## Raw quote arbitrage

Model-free checks on the OTM quote set, each run twice: on mid prices, and on
executable prices (buy at the ask, sell at the bid). For strike checks, OTM puts are
expressed as calls through put-call parity. Parity adds the same linear function of K
to bid and ask, so butterflies are unaffected, and call checks on converted puts are
exactly the corresponding put checks. Only the pair or triple that straddles F
depends on the estimated forward. Butterflies use strike-gap weights, because
spacing is uneven (5, 10, 25 and 50 point grids all appear).

**What the 620 mid violations are.** Most are rounding. The median mid butterfly
violation is $0.05 (one tick), and 56% of mid-only violations are 5 cents or less.

this means nothing.

**The ITM diagnostic.** Running the same strike checks on every clean quote, including
in-the-money strikes up to 10% ITM, gives 908 mid violations and 126 survivors, with
"profits" up to $22,700 per contract. These are plainly bad data. For example, the
Feb-2027 8350 put is quoted 530.4/532.8 while the 8325 put is 601.1/606.9, and the
8350 has zero open interest. ITM quotes in the Yahoo feed are unreliable, which is a
second reason (besides liquidity) to build the surface from OTM options only.

## Fits

Both fits minimise weighted squared error in total variance. Weights are the inverse
of the bid-ask spread in total variance. For SSVI each slice's weights are normalised
to mean 1 and its residuals are scaled by that slice's ATM total variance, so every
expiry counts about equally in vol terms. That scaling doesn't change the optimum of
a single SVI slice, so both models minimise the same objective.

* **SVI**: raw parameterisation, quasi-explicit calibration (Zeliade 2009). For fixed
  (m, σ) the slice is linear in (a, bσ, ρbσ), solved by constrained weighted least
  squares. Only (m, σ) are searched, with Nelder–Mead from 12 starts. Constraints:
  b ≥ 0, |ρ| < 1, σ > 0, a + bσ√(1−ρ²) ≥ 0, and b(1+|ρ|) ≤ 1.999. The last one keeps
  both asymptotic wing slopes, b(1±ρ), strictly below Lee's bound of 2.
* **SSVI**: power-law φ(θ) = η / (θ^γ (1+θ)^(1−γ)). One global (ρ, η, γ) plus one θ
  per expiry, fitted jointly with bounded least squares. No-arbitrage holds by
  construction: η = 2s/(1+|ρ|) with s ∈ (0, 1], γ ∈ (0, ½], and θ is a cumulative sum
  of non-negative increments.
* **SSVI (unconstrained)**: same, with γ ∈ (0, 1) and no η bound. This run is only a
  diagnostic, to measure what the constraints cost.

### Fitted arbitrage

A slice is free of butterfly arbitrage if and only if g(k) ≥ 0 for every k *and*
d₊(k) → −∞ as k → +∞ (GJ14 Lemma 2.2). The second condition holds exactly when the
right wing's asymptotic total-variance slope is below Lee's bound of 2. Both are
checked: g(k) on a grid k ∈ [−2.5, 1.0], and the wing slope analytically. Calendar
arbitrage is checked by whether adjacent fitted slices cross (w at T₂ below w at T₁)
on the same grid. Adjacent pairs are enough, because non-crossing is transitive.

| Model | Params | RMSE (vol pts) | Slices with g < 0 | …inside quoted k range | Wing slope ≥ 2 | Adjacent pairs crossing | …inside quoted k range |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SVI | 50 | 0.16 | 5 of 10 | 0 | 0 | 6 of 9 | 0 |
| SSVI | 13 | 3.00 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| SSVI unconstrained | 13 | 1.93 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

**A correction.** The first version of this pipeline bounded SVI wings at
b(1+|ρ|) ≤ 4. That was wrong: in total variance, Lee's moment formula caps the
asymptotic slope at 2 (GJ14 Remark 4.3), and Lemma 2.2 needs the right slope strictly
below 2. Under the old bound, 7 of the 10 SVI slices had right-wing slopes near 4.
By the full Lemma 2.2 test, 9 of 10 slices then had butterfly arbitrage, not the 5
originally reported. With the corrected bound, the same 7 slices sit on it (slope
1.999), and RMSE moves from 0.163 to 0.164 vol points. The data barely constrain the
far call wing, so tightening it costs almost nothing in fit.

Every SVI arbitrage sits on the **call wing, beyond the last quoted strike**. g turns
negative from k = 0.31 to 0.96 onward, depending on the slice (min g = −0.92 on 2027-01-15).
Slices cross from k = 0.16 to 0.51 onward. The cause is visible in the parameters:
for nine of ten expiries SVI puts its vertex to the right of the data (m = 0.19–1.01,
against a last quoted k of 0.07–0.41) with ρ > 0. On this snapshot the quoted smile is
still falling at the last liquid call, so the data never pins the right wing. SVI is
free to bend it however it likes. Seven slices push the wing all the way to the
Lee bound, and it bends hard. Inside the quoted range the SVI surface is clean, but as soon as you
extrapolate, for pricing or for a density, it is not.

## The trade-off

**SVI fits each smile about 18 times more tightly than SSVI (0.16 vs 3.00 vol points)
and keeps 80% of fitted vols inside the bid-ask spread, against 9% for SSVI. But half
of the SVI slices imply a negative density, and two thirds of adjacent expiry pairs
cross, all in the extrapolated call wing. SSVI uses 13 parameters instead of 50 and is
arbitrage-free everywhere by construction, at the cost of missing the short-dated
put wing by several vol points.**

Splitting SSVI's 3.0-point gap:

* About **1.1 points** comes from the no-arbitrage constraints. Both bind: γ = 0.500 sits
  on its bound and η(1+|ρ|) = 2.000 sits on its bound. Relaxing them gives 1.93.
* About **1.9 points** is structural, from a single ρ and a single φ for every maturity.
  Relaxing the constraints does not remove it.

The unconstrained fit lands at γ = 0.63, η(1+|ρ|) = 1.41. That breaks the sufficient
butterfly condition θφ²(1+|ρ|) ≤ 4 for θ < 0.015, which covers the seven shortest
expiries; the worst value at a fitted θ is 10.5. Even so, a direct check finds g ≥ 0
on k ∈ [−15, 15] for every fitted slice. On this snapshot the unconstrained surface
happens to be arbitrage-free. But that is something you learn by checking after the
fit, not something the parameterisation guarantees. The constrained fit is
guaranteed.

If you need a smile that matches quotes for marking or for spotting quote-level
relative value, SVI per slice is the better tool, but its wings must be fixed or
checked before any extrapolation. If you need a surface you can safely interpolate,
extrapolate, take densities from or feed to a local-vol model, SSVI's guarantee is
worth roughly 3 vol points of fit on short expiries. GJ14 §5.2 sketches a way to
get both: start from a square-root SVI fit, then refit SVI slice by slice with a heavy
penalty on "crossedness" (how far adjacent slices cross). That is out of scope here.

## No-arbitrage conditions used for SSVI

From Gatheral and Jacquier (2014), *Arbitrage-free SVI volatility surfaces*,
Quantitative Finance 14(1). Checked against the SSRN version dated 17 March 2013
(ssrn.com/abstract=2033323):

1. **Calendar** (Thm 4.1, necessary and sufficient): θ_t is non-decreasing in t, and
   0 ≤ ∂_θ(θφ(θ)) ≤ (1/ρ²)(1 + √(1−ρ²)) φ(θ) for all θ > 0, with no upper bound when ρ = 0.
2. **Butterfly** (Thm 4.2, sufficient): θφ(θ)(1+|ρ|) < 4 and θφ(θ)²(1+|ρ|) ≤ 4 for all θ > 0.
   Lemma 4.2 shows the first condition is necessary and the second is tight.

For the power law φ(θ) = η θ^(−γ) (1+θ)^(γ−1):

* θφ = η (θ/(1+θ))^(1−γ), which is increasing in θ and below η. So ∂_θ(θφ) ≥ 0, and
  ∂_θ(θφ)/φ = (1−γ)/(1+θ) < 1 ≤ (1+√(1−ρ²))/ρ². Calendar therefore needs only
  monotone θ.
* θφ(1+|ρ|) < η(1+|ρ|) ≤ 2 < 4.
* θφ² = η² (θ/(1+θ))^(1−2γ) / (1+θ) ≤ η² when γ ≤ ½. So θφ²(1+|ρ|) ≤ η²(1+|ρ|) ≤
  4/(1+|ρ|) ≤ 4 when η(1+|ρ|) ≤ 2.

The project brief's conditions (γ ∈ (0, ½], η(1+|ρ|) ≤ 2, θ increasing) therefore
imply both theorems. This φ is the paper's equation (4.5) in Remark 4.4, which says it
gives a surface "completely free of static arbitrage provided that η(1+|ρ|) ≤ 2"; the
SSRN text states no restriction on γ at that point. The derivation above needs γ ≤ ½
for the second butterfly condition: for γ > ½, θφ² grows without bound as θ → 0. That
matches the paper's own analysis of the pure power law in the same remark, and it is
exactly what the unconstrained fit (γ = 0.63) runs into. `tests/test_ssvi.py` also confirms this numerically:

* for 20 random admissible parameter sets, g ≥ 0 and slices never cross;
* fits to data generated outside the admissible region always return admissible
  parameters;
* parameters outside the region do produce g < 0.

## Limitations

* **One delayed weekend snapshot.** The two saved snapshots hold the same Friday
  quotes. Counts and fit errors will move day to day. The pipeline is built to be
  rerun on new snapshots (`python -m volsurf collect`).
* **Quote quality.** Yahoo bid and ask carry no timestamps or sizes, so "survives bid
  and ask" means "survives the displayed quotes", not "was executable".
* **Narrow call wing.** After cleaning, OTM calls reach only k ≈ 0.05–0.41. That is
  exactly why SVI's right wing is unconstrained.
* **Calendar check interpolation.** Raw calendar checks interpolate the longer expiry's
  total variance linearly in k between neighbouring quotes no more than 0.05 apart. It
  found no violations, even on mid.
* **Fitted checks are grid-based.** k ∈ [−2.5, 1.0] with 3,501 points; the wider
  [−15, 15] check was run once for the SSVI fits.
* **Rates.** Discount factors come from parity per expiry, not from an external OIS
  curve. They span 4.5–5.2% past one month, and 3.8% at two weeks.
